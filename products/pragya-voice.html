The room, minute four

Nobody buys a voice agent because it sounds human.

Every vendor demos well. By minute four the demo is over and three questions are left on the table — and they are not about the voice. Pragya Voice was built backwards from these three.

The fear

“What happens the day it calls the wrong person?”

The answer

It can't. Six checks run in the instant before the carrier is touched — not at upload, not at approval, at dial. A number that became ineligible after you approved the campaign is not dialled.

Every refusal is named, logged and countable. You can show a regulator the calls that didn't happen.

The fear

“When Legal asks what it said, what do I show them?”

The answer

The transcript, turn by turn, with the timing. The recording. The disclosure, spoken aloud before recording started. The opt-out, written the moment it was said — during the call, not in a nightly batch.

Every intent label carries a verbatim quote that is checked back against the transcript before you ever see it.

The fear

“How many months until this is actually running?”

The answer

One week from the first conversation to an evidence pack you can take to your board. No CRM integration, no prompt engineering, no IT ticket.

A spreadsheet and one sentence describing what you want to find out. That is the whole input. The regulatory registration needed to dial customers in India runs alongside it, openly, from day one.

The feature nobody else leads with

It says no. Out loud, in writing, six ways.

Most platforms treat compliance as a setting. Here it is a gate that physically precedes the carrier request, and it returns a machine-readable reason every time it closes. This is the part your risk team will want to read.

Approval is a signature, not a checkbox

No call is authorised by a flag the caller sends. A dial is authorised by a server-issued, single-use token bound to one destination — so nothing between the human saying yes and the phone ringing can substitute a different number.

Approving a campaign freezes it

Recipients, objective, caller identity, pitch, voice, language policy, retry rules and schedule are versioned at the moment you approve. Change any of them and it becomes a new version that needs approving again. What you signed off is what dials.

The actual product

The call is the cost. The evidence is the product.

Twenty seconds after hangup, every conversation resolves into the same structured object — no CRM data entry, no listening back, no “the rep said it went well”.

A decision

One of six labels: high, medium or low interest, not interested, call back, or escalate — with a calibrated confidence figure beside it.

The proof

A verbatim quote from the customer that supports the label, matched back against the stored transcript. If it can't be matched, you're told that too.

The next move

A next action, a follow-up date, and a one-line note written for the human who picks it up — what to say, and what not to repeat.

The context

Executive summary, what the customer actually wants in their own framing, sentiment, openings worth acting on, and objections a human should know before dialling back.

Your dashboard reports the number that matters

Not calls made. Cost per high-intent lead, alongside connect rate, engaged rate, and a funnel that separates attempted, connected, engaged, high-intent and escalated. Cost is accumulated from real usage on every call, not estimated.

Watch it happen, or don't

Live transcript as the call runs, a timeline of every event, and the recording afterwards. Campaigns show what's queued, what's blocked and why, what's been attempted and what's been analysed — as separate numbers, because collapsing them into one percentage is how progress starts lying to you.

Nothing is translated away

Telugu stays in Telugu script. Hindi stays in Devanagari. Words, order, punctuation and casing are stored as they were spoken — because a translated transcript is a paraphrase, and a paraphrase is not evidence.

Your business summary still arrives in English.

Interruptible, like a person

Speak over the agent and it stops inside roughly a third of a second, discards what it was about to say, and remembers only the part you actually heard. That last detail is the one most systems skip, and it's why they repeat themselves after being interrupted.

Both directions

It calls out, and it answers back.

One conversation engine, one evidence record, one set of disclosure and opt-out rules — pointed in both directions. What changes between them is the opening line and which regulations bite. What does not change is that every call still has to answer for itself.

Available now

Outbound — campaigns and single calls

Approval-frozen campaigns over a list you own, single calls placed by a person, and calls triggered from your own tools. Sequential by design: one live conversation at a time, because the alternative is predictive dialling and we are not building that.

This is the path with the regulatory weight on it, which is why every gate on this page exists.

Available now

Inbound — answering the calls you receive

Callers who ring your published number are greeted, qualified and captured in their own words, and hand a human the same evidence object an outbound call produces — the label, the verbatim quote, the recording and the next action.

The opening is written differently, because an inbound caller has already chosen to speak to you: consent and calling windows do not apply. The spoken AI disclosure and the recording notice still do, and still come before anything is recorded.

The two directions carry different obligations, and the platform knows which is which

An outbound commercial call is regulated communication: it needs a registered sender, recorded consent, a permitted calling window and a scrubbed number — so the six gates on this page run before the carrier is touched. An inbound call is a person choosing to reach you. Those gates do not apply, and we do not pretend they do.

What holds in both directions is the part that concerns the person on the line rather than the paperwork: they are told they are speaking to an AI before anything is recorded, and an opt-out is honoured the moment it is spoken.

India · who is allowed to call

We will not place calls to your customers on your behalf.

That sentence costs us the easiest demo in this market, and we mean it. Under TRAI's TCCCPR framework a commercial call belongs to a registered Principal Entity, dialled through registered telemarketing resources, against consent recorded in your name. A vendor who offers to skip that by putting your pitch on their own carrier account is handing you an unsolicited-commercial-communication exposure and keeping the convenience.

You are the sender of record

  • The Principal Entity registered on DLT, in your own name
  • The 140-series calling identity the recipient sees
  • The consent — held by you, evidenced by you, revocable to you
  • The carrier account the traffic runs on
  • The controller of your contacts' personal data under the DPDP Act
  • The counsel who signs off before customer dialling begins

We are the platform underneath it

  • The conversation engine, the gates, and the evidence record
  • DND and consent scrubbing executed on your records, before dial

The product enforces this rather than promising it

A campaign containing Indian numbers will not run until your readiness is recorded in the system. Until then the only door open is an operator test mode capped at a handful of numbers you own yourself — and it structurally cannot carry a customer list. That gate was built before this page existed, for our own protection as much as yours.

The 2025 amendment to TCCCPR also brings AI-generated voice under artificial-voice and auto-dialler rules, with notification duties attached. That is precisely why the disclosure on this platform is spoken aloud before recording begins and cannot be switched off.

This describes how we structure engagements. It is not legal advice, and it does not replace your own counsel's review — which is a step in the onboarding path above rather than an afterthought.
